Theoretical & computational molecular spectroscopy

Ketan Sharma

About me

I study molecular spectra and the interactions between electronic, vibrational, rotational, and spin degrees of freedom.

I develop theoretical models and computational methods for molecules with Jahn–Teller and pseudo-Jahn–Teller interactions. My work combines symmetry-adapted Hamiltonians, quantum chemistry, and numerical methods to connect molecular structure and dynamics with experimentally observable spectra.

My research also includes machine-learning approaches to quasi-diabatic potential energy surfaces, spin–phonon relaxation in magnetic materials, and the dynamics of molecular rotors in external fields.

Background

I completed my Ph.D. in 2016 at the Fritz Haber Institute of the Max Planck Society in Berlin, working with Bretislav Friedrich on molecular rotors in external fields and their applications to quantum computation. Before that, I earned my M.Sc. in Chemistry from the Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur in 2011.

My postdoctoral research has included work with Terry A. Miller at The Ohio State University on Jahn–Teller-active molecules, with John F. Stanton at the University of Florida on vibronic coupling and symmetry-adapted potentials, and with Xiaoguang Zhang on spin decoherence in magnetic materials.
