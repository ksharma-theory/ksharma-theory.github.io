Research

Theoretical and computational approaches to molecular spectra, coupled quantum states, and spin dynamics.

Vibronic coupling & molecular spectroscopy

Molecules beyond the Born–Oppenheimer approximation

I develop theoretical and computational descriptions of molecules with Jahn–Teller and pseudo-Jahn–Teller interactions. My work includes spin-vibronic spectra of the methoxy radical, vibronic coupling in calcium alkoxides, and rotational signatures of Jahn–Teller distortions.

This includes first-principles calculations of rotational distortion parameters, effective rotational Hamiltonians for molecules with threefold and fivefold symmetry axes, and the interpretation of experimentally observable spectroscopic parameters.

Potential energy surfaces & numerical methods

Symmetry-adapted vibronic Hamiltonians

I develop machine-learning approaches to high-order quasi-diabatic potential energy surfaces, with molecular symmetry used to organize and simplify the potential expansion. Related work addresses higher-order potential representations, including artificial “canyons,” and symmetry-based characterization of vibronic eigenvectors.

My computational work includes DVR treatments of vibronically coupled problems and Krylov–Schur methods for large sparse eigenvalue problems. These methods support the development of SOCJT for spin-vibronic calculations.

Spin–phonon coupling

Spin decoherence in magnetic materials

I study the role of spin–phonon coupling in decoherence near clock transitions. This work includes an angular-momentum-based coupling model and analytic expressions for T₁ and T₂ using Redfield theory, Bloch-equation relations, and a classical description of phonon displacements.

Molecular quantum dynamics

Molecular rotors in external fields

My earlier research studied polar paramagnetic molecules in combined electric, magnetic, and non-resonant optical fields; dipole-coupled molecular rotors; and proposals for quantum computing with ultracold molecular arrays. This included directional properties, adiabatic tracking of eigenstates, qubit entanglement, and optically controlled CNOT-gate schemes.
